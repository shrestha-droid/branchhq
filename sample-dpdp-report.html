Check5. No record of consent is kept

Section 6(10): where consent is the basis, the company must be able to prove it

Personal data is stored, but no table or field records when and to what each person agreed. Data people hand over for a service they asked for can rest on a legitimate use (Section 7) instead; anything else (marketing, analytics, sharing) needs consent you can prove, and a checkbox is not proof unless the answer is saved.

What to do: Save each consent with the person, the notice version, the purpose and the time, and record withdrawals the same way.

Should fix6. No way for people to delete their data

Sections 6(4) and 12(3): withdraw consent and have the data erased

No account deletion or data erasure was found in the code. People can ask for both, and must be able to withdraw consent as easily as they gave it.

What to do: Add a "delete my account" step that removes the person's data (not only hides it), including copies sent to other services, and keep a record that it was done.

The "students" table looks like it holds data about students or children, and no parental consent or age check was found. The site also loads Google Analytics and Meta Pixel.

Should fix8. Government IDs, bank or health details kept without encryption

Section 8(5) and Rule 6: encryption, masking or tokens as security safeguards

Aadhaar number, PAN and bank account are stored as ordinary fields, and no encryption, hashing or masking of them was found. Anyone who reads the database reads them. UIDAI also expects Aadhaar numbers to be kept encrypted in a separate vault.
