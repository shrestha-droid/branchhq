What you get

  • A map of your personal data: every kind your code stores (names, emails, phone numbers, Aadhaar, PAN, bank details, health, location, device IDs), the table it lives in, and the file and line.
  • Who receives it: every company your code sends data to (analytics, advertising, AI models, email, SMS, payments, chat), where each is based, and what personal data sits next to each call.
  • Your notice against your code: the companies and the kinds of data your privacy notice never mentions, read from the notice on your website. This is where a complaint or an enterprise customer's review will look first.
  • Your live site, before anyone clicks "accept": which analytics, advertising and session-recording tools start loading the moment a visitor arrives.
  • Gaps against the Act and Rules, each with its section: tracking before consent, no proof of consent, no way to delete an account, children's data without parental consent, Aadhaar, PAN or bank details kept unencrypted, personal data in server logs, and nothing that deletes old data.
  • Security safeguards (Section 8(5)): the full security review of the same code, because a breach is where the largest penalties are.
  • A record of processing as a spreadsheet, with every field already listed, for your lawyer or DPO to complete with purposes, legal basis and retention.

Why not just ask an AI to read the code?

  • Every statement can be checked. Each one points to a file and a line your engineer can open. Nothing in the report is a model's guess.
  • Same code, same answer. The rules are fixed, so next month's check is comparable with this one, and the report shows exactly what changed: new data, new companies, gaps closed.
  • Your code stays private. The review runs on a machine we control, or on your own computer with the Branch HQ app. No AI company reads your code to produce it.
  • A record no one can backdate. Each check goes into a tamper-evident log with an independent timestamp, so you can show when each gap was found and when you closed it. When the Data Protection Board sets a penalty, it must weigh whether you acted, and how quickly (Section 33(2)(e)). A record started later cannot show that.
